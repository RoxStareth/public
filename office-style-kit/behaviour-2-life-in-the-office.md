This is the second of two optional behaviour emails. It describes how the agents move and talk in the office picture. As before: read it, tell me what overlaps with what you already have, what you would add, and wait for my go. Keep every existing behaviour working.

STATUS OF AN AGENT
Every agent is always in exactly one of four states, worked out from the board of work:
- Working: it has an open card, or its role is to be always on.
- Waiting on you: it has a question waiting for my answer. It stands still at its desk and shows a red round alert mark with an exclamation point that hops 3 pixels, and its bubble reads "tag: hourglass" with its next step underneath.
- Proposing ideas: nothing is open and nothing is waiting, so it pitches ideas to the team.
- Parked: its scope has not been agreed yet. It shows a sleeping bubble and a dimmed screen on its monitor.
The roster tile for each agent shows a coloured badge square with its short tag, its name with level tag, a status pill (working in green, waiting on you in amber, proposing ideas in purple, parked in grey), and one line of what it is doing now.

NOW AND NEXT
For each agent, "now" is the title of its first open card; if it has none, it is "Waiting on your answer", "Parked until scope is agreed", "Pitching ideas to the team", or a role-specific idle line. "Next" is the next card in line, or "Whatever the board needs".

WALKING
- Speed about 34 pixels per second, 1.6 times faster inside the lift. Facing follows the direction of travel. The walk cycles through the four leg frames about every 0.14 seconds. When an agent is typing at its desk it alternates between two frames every 0.3 seconds and faces up.
- Routes: out of the room through the front, along the corridor, into another room's door, or to the lift and up or down, then out along the next corridor. While someone is in the lift a grey lift car is drawn around them.
- Idle behaviour is chosen by weighted random choice each time an agent is free (wait 0.6 to 2 seconds between plans). Working agents mostly type at the desk for 4 to 9 seconds, or walk an errand: hand finished work to the builder, ask the checker to check a card, ask another role for input, or visit the project manager. The project manager sometimes lines up a card for an agent or checks the inbox of things waiting on me. Idle agents with nothing to do may meet in the meeting room: two stand at facing spots for about 3 seconds, one with a light-bulb bubble and the other with a speech bubble, and the ticker records "A and B swapped what they've learned". A resting agent may lie on a bed asleep for 14 to 28 seconds with a sleeping bubble, or play a game at its desk with a flickering screen.
- An errand has the visitor walk to the host's door, stand a moment with an emoji bubble (for example a clipboard or a magnifying glass), the host answers with a quick reply bubble of two or three emojis for 2.6 seconds, then the visitor walks home and sits down facing up.
- When everything is waiting on me, nobody walks; everyone stays at their desk. When I pause the whole office, all movement and all animation stop.

OBJECTS THAT REFLECT REAL STATE
- Monitors flicker when their owner is working.
- A wall board in the project manager's room shows five columns (backlog, ready, doing, waiting on me, done), each with up to four tiny coloured bars for the number of cards in it.
- Each agent's wall whiteboard shows up to three goals as small ticks that turn green once the agent has finished that many cards.
- My own room has an inbox: a small desk phone that rings (blinks yellow with little sound lines) when something is waiting, a stack of paper that grows with the number of waiting items (up to six), and a red exclamation mark that blinks.
- My own bubble reads "Me: phone N" when N items wait, or "Me: coffee" when none do.

DIALOGUE AND MENU BOXES
Selecting an agent opens a dialogue box over the bottom of the picture, in the retro text-box style from the style emails: the agent's name and its question typed out letter by letter, a line "Recommended: ..." with its suggestion, a link to answer on the board, a small close button and a bouncing "more" arrow at the bottom right. A menu box at the top lists things like the team, the rules and the status report, with a bouncing triangle on the hovered or focused item and a green tick on done items.

OFFICE RULES POSTERS (optional)
A meeting room wall can carry five rule posters that open in a side panel when clicked: public data only; work until blocked then stop; choose, log and carry on; ideas every day; levelling. The wording of the levelling poster is in behaviour 1.

PAUSE AND MOTION
